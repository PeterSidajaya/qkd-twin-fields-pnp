\section{Current picture}

The project has converged to a fairly clear security story.

The original guessing-probability / min-entropy route appears too loose for the
protocol and has been set aside. The current route is a phase-error SDP based
on the Gram-matrix method of arXiv:1901.01942. With only a constraint on the
\emph{mean} incoming energy, Eve retains a strong degree of freedom: she can
send mostly vacuum together with rare bright pulses. Channel loss makes this
attack progressively more effective because the observed pass probability
decreases with distance, allowing Eve to concentrate the same average energy
into fewer and brighter accepted rounds.

Strong attenuation alone does not remove this freedom. In the strong-
attenuation limit it is more natural to describe Eve's source by a distribution
of delivered energies \(u\), giving a mixed-Poisson description. This
reformulation makes the concentration attack transparent.

The most promising fix so far is \emph{per-pulse energy monitoring}: accept
only pulses whose incoming energy lies inside a prescribed window (or use
feed-forward attenuation to normalize accepted pulses). In the current
numerics this increases the secure range from roughly \(2\) km to somewhere
between \(20\) km and \(40\) km.

The immediate task is no longer to search for a new attack mechanism. It is to
turn the per-pulse-monitoring picture into a rigorous security statement and
then produce publication-quality numerics.

\section{Protocol and security model}

The central node is untrusted and supplies the optical pulses. Alice and Bob
apply phase modulation and send the light back to the node for interference
and detection. Eve is therefore allowed to control both the source and the
measurement device; Alice and Bob trust only their local modulation,
attenuation, and monitoring.

The phase encoding uses \(M\) bases,
\[
    \phi_x = \frac{x\pi}{M},
    \qquad x\in\{0,\ldots,M-1\},
\]
with the bit value contributing an additional phase shift \(a\pi\). In the
present phase-error formulation, basis \(x=0\) is used for key generation and
the remaining \(M-1\) bases are test bases.

The working key-rate expression is
\begin{equation}
    R = P_{\rm pass}
    \left[
        1-h_2(e_{\rm ph})
        -f_{\rm ec}h_2(e_{\rm bit})
    \right].
\end{equation}
For the current numerical choice
\[
    e_{\rm bit}=0.015,
    \qquad
    f_{\rm ec}=1.15,
\]
positive key requires approximately
\[
    e_{\rm ph}<0.292.
\]

The phase-error objective is the analogue of Eq.~(A7) of
arXiv:1901.01942: \(e_{\rm ph}\) is an affine function of off-diagonal
entries of the Gram matrix of Eve's post-measurement states. The test bases
are essential. Without them the complementary-basis phase error is
unconstrained and the SDP returns the trivial value \(e_{\rm ph}=1/2\).

\section{Route that was abandoned: guessing probability}

The guessing-probability SDP was corrected in two important ways.

First, the photon-number tail / energy constraint was fixed so that probability
mass outside the numerical cutoff is charged consistently to the combined
energy budget. Second, Eve's final bit guess was allowed to depend on the
basis after that basis is publicly announced, while her earlier announcement
remains basis-independent.

After those corrections, for
\[
    \bar n = 10^{-2},
    \qquad L=0,
    \qquad M=4,
\]
the SDP gives
\[
    P_{\rm guess}=0.9336.
\]
A positive key from that min-entropy bound would require
\[
    P_{\rm guess}<0.9145.
\]
Thus the corrected guessing-probability route gives zero key already at zero
distance. Since the phase-error calculation gives positive key in the same
regime, this is interpreted as looseness of the min-entropy bound rather than
as evidence that the protocol itself is insecure.

\section{Validation of the phase-error implementation}

Two checks have been used.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item The implementation reproduces the trusted-source finite-test-state
    phase-matching calculation of Fig.~6(a) of arXiv:1901.01942. The
    reproduced PLOB crossing is about \(48\) dB, compared with roughly
    \(50\) dB in the published figure.

    \item If the plug-and-play source is artificially pinned to the same
    coherent-state distribution, two independent implementations agree to
    about five digits. This checks the overlap construction, the loss
    convention, the truncation, and the indexing in the phase-error objective.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Stage 1: untrusted source with only mean-energy monitoring}

With only a mean-energy constraint on Eve's source, the test bases do constrain
the phase structure. At
\[
    \bar n=10^{-2},
    \qquad L=0,
    \qquad N_{\max}=7,
\]
the current results are
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ccc}
\toprule
\(M\) & number of test states & \(e_{\rm ph}\) \\
\midrule
2 & 2 & 0.5000 \\
3 & 4 & 0.2865 \\
4 & 6 & 0.2573 \\
5 & 8 & 0.2572 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
so the improvement essentially saturates by \(M=4\).

Nevertheless, the key rate disappears by about \(2\) km. Inspecting Eve's
optimum shows that the problem is not a failure of the test bases to constrain
relative phase. Rather, Eve exploits freedom in the photon-number
distribution. A representative optimum has a very large
\[
    g^{(2)}\sim 36,
\]
whereas a coherent state has \(g^{(2)}=1\). A first-moment energy constraint
does not control this bunching.

\section{Stage 2: physical attenuation}

\subsection{What changes under attenuation}

Let the trusted local attenuation have transmissivity
\[
    \tau=t^2.
\]
If the incoming state is a Fock state \(\lvert m\rangle\), then after pure loss
the transmitted photon number is binomially distributed,
\[
    N\sim{\rm Binomial}(m,\tau).
\]
For two phase encodings that differ by \(\Delta\phi\), the relevant transmitted
overlap is therefore
\begin{equation}
    \Lambda_m(\Delta\phi)
    =
    \left(
        1-\tau+\tau e^{i\Delta\phi}
    \right)^m.
    \label{eq:fock-overlap}
\end{equation}

Numerically, at
\[
    \bar n=10^{-2},\qquad L=0,\qquad M=4,\qquad N_{\rm in}=60,
\]
one finds approximately
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{cc}
\toprule
\(\tau=t^2\) & \(e_{\rm ph}\) \\
\midrule
1.0 & 0.2567 \\
0.5 & 0.2293 \\
0.1 & 0.2094 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
so attenuation genuinely helps the phase-error bound.

\subsection{Why the mixed-state obstruction of arXiv:2012.11104 is not fatal here}

Section~V of arXiv:2012.11104 points out a real obstruction in the general
channel-discrimination problem: after loss, the received states are mixed, so
the original pure-state Gram construction is unavailable, while the mixed
states are not diagonal in a known common basis that would make the
source-discrimination construction applicable.

Our situation has extra symmetry. Pure loss is phase covariant. If
\(W_s\) denotes the phase modulation corresponding to symbol \(s\), then the
attenuated encoded states obey
\begin{equation}
    \rho_s = W_s \rho_0 W_s^\dagger
\end{equation}
for one common mixed state \(\rho_0\).

Choose one purification \(\lvert\chi\rangle_{TR}\) of \(\rho_0\). A
consistent purification of the entire encoded family is then
\begin{equation}
    \lvert\chi_s\rangle
    =
    (W_s\otimes I_R)\lvert\chi\rangle.
\end{equation}
Hence
\begin{align}
    \langle\chi_{s_1}\vert\chi_{s_2}\rangle
    &=
    \operatorname{Tr}
    \left[
        \rho_0 W_{s_1}^\dagger W_{s_2}
    \right].
\end{align}
Because the phase operators are diagonal in photon number, this quantity is
determined by the attenuated photon-number distribution.

The important point is therefore not that mixed states can always be handled
by purification. The important point is that \emph{all of our mixed states
form a unitary orbit of one common state}, so a single common purification can
be chosen consistently.

There is also a subtle but important ordering issue. One should first trace
out the trusted loss port and then purify the transmitted reduced state as a
mathematical device. If one instead keeps the physical reflected port as part
of the purification, the beamsplitter is unitary and the global overlaps are
identical to the input overlaps. In that global description attenuation
cannot improve distinguishability at all, which is not the object relevant to
the untrusted node.

\subsection{Why this still does not solve the protocol}

For realistic plug-and-play attenuation one may need
\[
    \tau\sim 10^{-6}
\]
or smaller. A direct Fock-grid treatment then requires a photon-number cutoff
of order \(1/\tau\), which is numerically impractical.

More importantly, attenuation does not remove the essential source freedom:
\(g^{(2)}\) is invariant under a uniform loss channel. Loss rescales pulse
energies but does not stop Eve from using a distribution with large
pulse-to-pulse fluctuations.

\section{Stage 3: strong-attenuation / continuum reformulation}

\subsection{Poisson limit of binomial thinning}

The natural variable in the strong-attenuation regime is the
\emph{delivered energy}
\begin{equation}
    u=\tau m.
\end{equation}
Substituting \(m=u/\tau\) into Eq.~\eqref{eq:fock-overlap} gives
\begin{equation}
    \Lambda
    =
    \left[
        1-\tau(1-e^{i\Delta\phi})
    \right]^{u/\tau}.
\end{equation}
Taking \(\tau\to0\) at fixed \(u\),
\begin{equation}
    \Lambda
    \longrightarrow
    \exp\left[-u(1-e^{i\Delta\phi})\right].
    \label{eq:poisson-kernel}
\end{equation}
This is the characteristic function of a Poisson random variable with mean
\(u\). Equivalently,
\[
    {\rm Binomial}(m,\tau)
    \longrightarrow
    {\rm Poisson}(m\tau).
\]

Thus a strongly attenuated \emph{fixed-energy component} becomes
Poissonian. An arbitrary untrusted source, however, is not forced to have one
fixed value of \(u\).

\subsection{Mixed-Poisson description}

Let Eve choose the delivered energy according to a probability measure
\(\nu(u)\). Conditional on \(u\),
\[
    N\mid u \sim {\rm Poisson}(u).
\]
The resulting source is therefore a \emph{mixture of Poisson distributions}.
A trusted coherent source is the special case
\[
    \nu(u)=\delta(u-u_0).
\]

For the two-arm problem the overlap kernel becomes
\begin{equation}
    \Lambda
    =
    \int d\nu(u_A,u_B)\,
    \exp\left[
        -u_A(1-e^{i\Delta\phi_A})
        -u_B(1-e^{i\Delta\phi_B})
    \right].
    \label{eq:mixed-poisson-kernel}
\end{equation}

This reformulation is useful in two ways. Computationally, the grid is now in
\(u=O(\bar n)\) rather than in the enormous input photon number
\(m=O(\bar n/\tau)\). Conceptually, Eve's remaining freedom is exposed as the
freedom to choose the mixing measure \(\nu\).

The second-order correlation of a mixed-Poisson source is
\begin{equation}
    g^{(2)}
    =
    \frac{\mathbb E[u^2]}{\mathbb E[u]^2}
    =
    1+\frac{{\rm Var}(u)}{\mathbb E[u]^2}.
\end{equation}
Mean-energy monitoring fixes only \(\mathbb E[u]\); it leaves
\({\rm Var}(u)\) essentially unrestricted. This is the precise sense in which
strong attenuation Poissonises each fixed component but does \emph{not}
turn an arbitrary source into a single coherent-state-like Poisson source.

At \(\tau=10^{-6}\), \(M=4\), and \(L=0\), optimizing the mean energy gives a
zero-distance optimum near
\[
    \bar n\simeq 0.1,
\]
with
\[
    e_{\rm ph}=0.2348,
    \qquad
    R=6.89\times10^{-3}.
\]
The zero-distance rate improves, but the distance cutoff remains around
\(2\) km.

\section{Physical diagnosis: the concentration attack}

The mixed-Poisson picture suggests a simple extremal strategy for Eve:
\begin{equation}
    \nu(u)
    =
    (1-p)\delta(u)
    +
    p\,\delta(u-u_0).
\end{equation}
That is, Eve sends vacuum most of the time and a bright pulse with probability
\(p\).

The mean-energy constraint per arm gives approximately
\begin{equation}
    p u_0 \le \frac{\bar n}{2}.
    \label{eq:mean-constraint}
\end{equation}
Since vacuum rounds do not account for the ordinary signal clicks, essentially
all successful rounds must come from the bright component. Therefore one
needs, parametrically,
\begin{equation}
    p \gtrsim P_{\rm pass}.
\end{equation}
Combining this with Eq.~\eqref{eq:mean-constraint},
\begin{equation}
    u_0
    \lesssim
    \frac{\bar n/2}{P_{\rm pass}}.
    \label{eq:u0-bound}
\end{equation}

In the weak-signal regime the honest pass probability scales roughly as
\[
    P_{\rm pass}\sim\eta\bar n,
\]
where \(\eta\) is the relevant channel transmission. Therefore
\begin{equation}
    u_0\sim O(\eta^{-1}),
\end{equation}
or, with the present per-arm convention,
\[
    u_0\lesssim \frac{1}{2\eta}.
\]

This scaling is the essential point. As distance increases,
\(P_{\rm pass}\) becomes smaller. Eve may then reduce \(p\) and increase
\(u_0\) while keeping the same average energy. The protocol keeps only click
rounds, so conditioning on a click selects the rare bright part of Eve's
mixture:
\[
    \Pr(\text{bright}\mid\text{click})\approx 1.
\]

For two distinct phase encodings, the magnitude of the strong-attenuation
overlap is
\begin{equation}
    \left|\Lambda(u_0,\Delta\phi)\right|
    =
    \exp\left[-u_0(1-\cos\Delta\phi)\right].
\end{equation}
Hence the encoded phase states become nearly orthogonal when \(u_0\) is large.
Eve can then discriminate them increasingly well, the complementary coherence
collapses, and the SDP can drive
\[
    e_{\rm ph}\rightarrow \frac12.
\]

This also explains why varying \(\bar n\) changes the zero-distance rate but
barely changes the range:
\[
    \frac{\bar n}{P_{\rm pass}}
    \sim
    \frac{1}{\eta}.
\]
The explicit \(\bar n\) approximately cancels from the concentration bound.

A better description than saying that the fibre is not degrading the signal
is:
\begin{quote}
The range collapse is not primarily caused by accumulated optical noise in the
accepted signal. Channel loss lowers the required pass probability, which
allows Eve to concentrate a fixed mean-energy budget into increasingly rare
and increasingly bright accepted rounds.
\end{quote}

\section{Stage 4: per-pulse energy monitoring}

The plug-and-play architecture gives a natural way to attack the concentration
freedom directly. Before Alice and Bob apply their very large attenuation, the
incoming pulse can be bright enough to measure accurately with a classical
photodiode.

Two possibilities are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item reject any pulse whose measured energy lies outside an allowed
    interval;
    \item use the measured input energy to feed forward to the attenuation, so
    that every accepted pulse has nearly fixed delivered energy.
\end{enumerate}

In the continuum SDP this changes the source constraint from a first-moment
condition to a support condition,
\begin{equation}
    u\in
    \left[
        u_\star(1-w),
        u_\star(1+w)
    \right]
\end{equation}
for every accepted round.

At
\[
    \bar n=0.1,
    \qquad
    L=0,
    \qquad
    M=4,
    \qquad
    \tau=10^{-6},
\]
the current results are
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
constraint & \(e_{\rm ph}\) & \(R\) \\
\midrule
mean only & 0.2348 & \(6.89\times10^{-3}\) \\
exact per-pulse energy & 0.1064 & \(3.11\times10^{-2}\) \\
\(\pm20\%\) window & 0.1099 & \(3.02\times10^{-2}\) \\
\(\pm50\%\) window & 0.1263 & \(2.64\times10^{-2}\) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Thus even a \(\pm20\%\) window gives about a \(4.4\times\) improvement in
the zero-distance rate.

For the same \(\pm20\%\) window,
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ccc}
\toprule
distance & \(e_{\rm ph}\) & \(R\) \\
\midrule
0 km  & 0.1099 & \(3.02\times10^{-2}\) \\
5 km  & 0.1440 & \(1.80\times10^{-2}\) \\
10 km & 0.1778 & \(1.02\times10^{-2}\) \\
20 km & 0.2473 & \(2.12\times10^{-3}\) \\
40 km & 0.5000 & \(0\) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
so the current secure-range cutoff moves from about \(2\) km to somewhere
between \(20\) and \(40\) km.

\section{Open points}

The main unresolved items are:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Rigorous post-selection argument.}
    The present SDP assumes that, after the local energy test, the conditional
    mixing measure for accepted rounds is supported inside the chosen energy
    window. This should be derived carefully as part of the security proof.

    \item \textbf{Generality of the phase-error formula.}
    Re-derive the analogue of Eq.~(A7) of arXiv:1901.01942 directly for an
    arbitrary untrusted Fock-diagonal source, rather than relying only on the
    coherent-state derivation and numerical cross-checks.

    \item \textbf{Solver accuracy.}
    The strongest continuum/window calculations currently return
    \texttt{optimal\_inaccurate} with CLARABEL. Publication-quality scans
    should be rerun with MOSEK or another higher-accuracy solver.

    \item \textbf{Use more observed statistics.}
    The current implementation constrains only an average QBER over bases.
    The Gram-matrix framework allows separate per-basis pass and error
    constraints, which should tighten the phase-error bound.

    \item \textbf{Revisit the number of test bases.}
    The apparent saturation near \(M=4\) was found in a regime dominated by
    the concentration attack. Once that attack is removed by per-pulse
    monitoring, the optimal value of \(M\) should be re-optimized.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Working conclusion}

The main conceptual mechanism is now understood. Mean-energy monitoring does
not control rare bright pulses, and channel loss turns that freedom into a
post-selection advantage for Eve. Strong attenuation makes the mathematics
cleaner, but by itself it only rescales the attack. Per-pulse energy monitoring
directly removes the degree of freedom responsible for the range collapse and
is therefore the current leading route toward a useful plug-and-play
phase-matching QKD protocol.
