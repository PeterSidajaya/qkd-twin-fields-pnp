\section{Current picture}

The project has converged to a fairly clear security story.

The original guessing-probability / min-entropy route appears too loose for the
protocol and has been set aside. The current route is a phase-error SDP based
on the Gram-matrix method of arXiv:1901.01942. With only a constraint on the
\emph{mean} incoming energy, Eve retains a strong degree of freedom: she can
send mostly vacuum together with rare bright pulses. Channel loss makes this
attack progressively more effective because the observed pass probability
decreases with distance, allowing Eve to concentrate the same average energy
into fewer and brighter accepted rounds.

Strong attenuation alone does not remove this freedom. In the strong-
attenuation limit it is more natural to describe Eve's source by a distribution
of delivered energies \(u\), giving a mixed-Poisson description. This
reformulation makes the concentration attack transparent.

The most promising fix so far is \emph{per-pulse energy monitoring}: accept
only pulses whose incoming energy lies inside a prescribed window (or use
feed-forward attenuation to normalize accepted pulses). In the current
numerics this increases the secure range from roughly \(2\) km to somewhere
between \(20\) km and \(40\) km.

The immediate task is no longer to search for a new attack mechanism. It is to
turn the per-pulse-monitoring picture into a rigorous security statement and
then produce publication-quality numerics.

\section{Protocol and security model}

The central node is untrusted and supplies the optical pulses. Alice and Bob
apply phase modulation and send the light back to the node for interference
and detection. Eve is therefore allowed to control both the source and the
measurement device; Alice and Bob trust only their local modulation,
attenuation, and monitoring.

The phase encoding uses \(M\) bases,
\[
    \phi_x = \frac{x\pi}{M},
    \qquad x\in\{0,\ldots,M-1\},
\]
with the bit value contributing an additional phase shift \(a\pi\). In the
present phase-error formulation, basis \(x=0\) is used for key generation and
the remaining \(M-1\) bases are test bases.

The working key-rate expression is
\begin{equation}
    R = P_{\rm pass}
    \left[
        1-h_2(e_{\rm ph})
        -f_{\rm ec}h_2(e_{\rm bit})
    \right].
\end{equation}
For the current numerical choice
\[
    e_{\rm bit}=0.015,
    \qquad
    f_{\rm ec}=1.15,
\]
positive key requires approximately
\[
    e_{\rm ph}<0.292.
\]

The phase-error objective is the analogue of Eq.~(A7) of
arXiv:1901.01942: \(e_{\rm ph}\) is an affine function of off-diagonal
entries of the Gram matrix of Eve's post-measurement states. The test bases
are essential. Without them the complementary-basis phase error is
unconstrained and the SDP returns the trivial value \(e_{\rm ph}=1/2\).

\section{Derivation of the phase-error objective for an arbitrary untrusted source}
\label{sec:a7-general}

Equation~(A7) of arXiv:1901.01942 was written there for coherent-state
phase encoding.  For the present protocol, however, the same algebraic
formula does not require the key states to be coherent, product, or even
prepared by Alice and Bob.  It follows from source replacement, the
announcement-dependent bit correction, and the definition of phase error on
the virtual qubits.

Let
\[
    P_A=e^{i\pi\hat n_A},
    \qquad
    P_B=e^{i\pi\hat n_B}.
\]
Eve may prepare an arbitrary pure state
\[
    |\Psi\rangle_{ABE_0},
\]
where \(E_0\) is retained by Eve.  The four key-basis states generated by
Alice's and Bob's bits \(a,b\in\{0,1\}\) are
\begin{equation}
    |\psi_{ab}\rangle
    =
    (P_A^a\otimes P_B^b\otimes I_{E_0})
    |\Psi\rangle.
    \label{eq:a7-key-states-general}
\end{equation}
No assumption on the photon-number distribution or on factorisation between
\(A\), \(B\), and \(E_0\) is made.

Define the \(X\)-basis labels
\[
    |x_0\rangle=|+\rangle,
    \qquad
    |x_1\rangle=|-\rangle.
\]
A source-replacement state for the actual random bit choices is
\begin{equation}
    |\Omega\rangle_{A_0B_0ABE_0}
    =
    \frac12
    \sum_{a,b=0}^1
    |x_a x_b\rangle_{A_0B_0}
    |\psi_{ab}\rangle_{ABE_0}.
    \label{eq:a7-source-replacement}
\end{equation}
Indeed,
\begin{equation}
    \operatorname{Tr}_{A_0B_0}
    |\Omega\rangle\!\langle\Omega|
    =
    \frac14
    \sum_{a,b}
    |\psi_{ab}\rangle\!\langle\psi_{ab}|,
    \label{eq:a7-eve-equivalence}
\end{equation}
which is exactly the state seen by Eve in the prepare-and-measure protocol
when the key bits are independent and uniform.  Measuring \(A_0B_0\) in the
\(X\) basis can therefore be delayed until after Eve's announcement.

Let Eve's most general attack, including the untrusted node, be represented
by an isometry
\begin{equation}
    V|\psi_{ab}\rangle
    =
    |e^+_{ab}\rangle_E|+\rangle_Z
    +
    |e^-_{ab}\rangle_E|-\rangle_Z
    +
    |e^\varnothing_{ab}\rangle_E|\varnothing\rangle_Z,
    \label{eq:a7-attack-isometry}
\end{equation}
where the vectors on \(E\) are sub-normalised and the orthogonal register
\(Z\) stores the public announcement.  The average key-basis pass probability
is
\begin{equation}
    P_{\rm pass}
    =
    \frac14
    \sum_{a,b}
    \left(
      \|e^+_{ab}\|^2+\|e^-_{ab}\|^2
    \right).
    \label{eq:a7-ppass-general}
\end{equation}

After projecting onto the successful outcomes, the normalised virtual state
is
\begin{equation}
    |\Omega_{\rm pass}\rangle
    =
    \frac{1}{2\sqrt{P_{\rm pass}}}
    \sum_{a,b}
    |x_a x_b\rangle
    \left(
      |e^+_{ab}\rangle|+\rangle_Z
      +
      |e^-_{ab}\rangle|-\rangle_Z
    \right).
    \label{eq:a7-pass-state}
\end{equation}
The protocol uses \(\Psi^+\) as the target Bell state.  For a \(+\)
announcement Bob does nothing; for a \(-\) announcement he flips his raw bit.
On the virtual qubit this is the Pauli \(Z_{B_0}\), since
\[
    Z|+\rangle=|-\rangle,
    \qquad
    Z|-\rangle=|+\rangle.
\]
After this controlled correction,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    |\widetilde\Omega_{\rm pass}\rangle
    =
    \frac{1}{2\sqrt{P_{\rm pass}}}
    \Big[&
    |++\rangle |F_{++}\rangle
    +|+-\rangle |F_{+-}\rangle\\
    &+|-+\rangle |F_{-+}\rangle
    +|--\rangle |F_{--}\rangle
    \Big],
    \label{eq:a7-corrected-state}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{align}
    |F_{++}\rangle
    &=
    |e^+_{++}\rangle|+\rangle_Z
    +
    |e^-_{+-}\rangle|-\rangle_Z,\\
    |F_{+-}\rangle
    &=
    |e^+_{+-}\rangle|+\rangle_Z
    +
    |e^-_{++}\rangle|-\rangle_Z,\\
    |F_{-+}\rangle
    &=
    |e^+_{-+}\rangle|+\rangle_Z
    +
    |e^-_{--}\rangle|-\rangle_Z,\\
    |F_{--}\rangle
    &=
    |e^+_{--}\rangle|+\rangle_Z
    +
    |e^-_{-+}\rangle|-\rangle_Z.
    \label{eq:a7-F-vectors}
\end{align}

The target state \(|\Psi^+\rangle\) is a \(+1\) eigenstate of
\(Y\otimes Y\).  Hence, if the phase error is the probability of obtaining
the wrong \(Y\)-basis parity,
\begin{equation}
    e_{\rm ph}
    =
    \frac{1-\langle Y_{A_0}\otimes Y_{B_0}\rangle}{2}.
    \label{eq:a7-phase-definition}
\end{equation}
In the \(X\) basis,
\[
    Y|+\rangle=-i|-\rangle,
    \qquad
    Y|-\rangle=i|+\rangle,
\]
and therefore
\begin{align}
    Y\otimes Y|++\rangle&=-|--\rangle,\\
    Y\otimes Y|--\rangle&=-|++\rangle,\\
    Y\otimes Y|+-\rangle&=|-+\rangle,\\
    Y\otimes Y|-+\rangle&=|+-\rangle.
\end{align}
Substituting Eq.~\eqref{eq:a7-corrected-state} gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \langle Y\otimes Y\rangle
    =
    \frac{1}{2P_{\rm pass}}
    \operatorname{Re}\Big[
       \langle F_{+-}|F_{-+}\rangle
       -
       \langle F_{++}|F_{--}\rangle
    \Big].
    \label{eq:a7-yy-F}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Because the announcement states \(|+\rangle_Z\) and \(|-\rangle_Z\) are
orthogonal,
\begin{align}
    \langle F_{++}|F_{--}\rangle
    &=
    \langle e^+_{++}|e^+_{--}\rangle
    +
    \langle e^-_{+-}|e^-_{-+}\rangle,\\
    \langle F_{+-}|F_{-+}\rangle
    &=
    \langle e^+_{+-}|e^+_{-+}\rangle
    +
    \langle e^-_{++}|e^-_{--}\rangle.
\end{align}
Combining these expressions with
Eq.~\eqref{eq:a7-phase-definition} yields
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
    e_{\rm ph}
    =
    \frac12
    +
    \frac{1}{4P_{\rm pass}}
    \operatorname{Re}\Big[
      &\langle e^+_{++}|e^+_{--}\rangle
      -
      \langle e^-_{++}|e^-_{--}\rangle\\
      &-
      \langle e^+_{+-}|e^+_{-+}\rangle
      +
      \langle e^-_{+-}|e^-_{-+}\rangle
    \Big].
\end{aligned}}
    \label{eq:a7-general}
\end{equation}
This is exactly Eq.~(A7) of arXiv:1901.01942.

\begin{proposition}[Generality of Eq.~(A7)]
Equation~\eqref{eq:a7-general} does not use coherent-state amplitudes,
Poisson photon statistics, product states, or trusted sources.  It is valid
for any four pure key-basis states \(\{|\psi_{ab}\rangle\}\), including the
joint states generated by local phase modulation of an arbitrary source
entangled with Eve, provided that the bits are uniform, the public successful
announcements are \(+\) and \(-\), and Bob applies the stated correction.
\end{proposition}

For the attenuated stages the states returned to Eve are mixed after the
trusted loss ports are traced out.  Choose the common purification introduced
in Section~\ref{sec:stage2-rigorous} and include its purifying register in the
residual vectors \(|e^z_{ab}\rangle\).  The derivation above is unchanged.
Optimising over arbitrary Gram matrices on that enlarged purifying space can
only enlarge the set of attacks relative to the physical one, so the resulting
phase-error optimum remains conservative.

Thus the coherent-state restriction of the original Appendix~A is not a
restriction on the algebraic phase-error objective used here.  The source
model affects the SDP through the admissible overlaps and observed
probabilities, not through the form of Eq.~\eqref{eq:a7-general}.


\section{Route that was abandoned: guessing probability}

The guessing-probability SDP was corrected in two important ways.

First, the photon-number tail / energy constraint was fixed so that probability
mass outside the numerical cutoff is charged consistently to the combined
energy budget. Second, Eve's final bit guess was allowed to depend on the
basis after that basis is publicly announced, while her earlier announcement
remains basis-independent.

After those corrections, for
\[
    \bar n = 10^{-2},
    \qquad L=0,
    \qquad M=4,
\]
the SDP gives
\[
    P_{\rm guess}=0.9336.
\]
A positive key from that min-entropy bound would require
\[
    P_{\rm guess}<0.9145.
\]
Thus the corrected guessing-probability route gives zero key already at zero
distance. Since the phase-error calculation gives positive key in the same
regime, this is interpreted as looseness of the min-entropy bound rather than
as evidence that the protocol itself is insecure.

\section{Validation of the phase-error implementation}

Two checks have been used.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item The implementation reproduces the trusted-source finite-test-state
    phase-matching calculation of Fig.~6(a) of arXiv:1901.01942. The
    reproduced PLOB crossing is about \(48\) dB, compared with roughly
    \(50\) dB in the published figure.

    \item If the plug-and-play source is artificially pinned to the same
    coherent-state distribution, two independent implementations agree to
    about five digits. This checks the overlap construction, the loss
    convention, the truncation, and the indexing in the phase-error objective.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Stage 1: untrusted source with only mean-energy monitoring}
\label{sec:stage1-rigorous}

This section writes the bare-source model as an exact infinite-dimensional
optimization first, and only afterwards introduces the finite-dimensional
relaxation used numerically. Keeping those two levels separate is important:
the SDP is rigorous only if every physical source and attack is mapped to a
feasible point of the numerical problem.

\subsection{Exact source overlaps}

Let \(A\) and \(B\) denote the optical modes entering Alice's and Bob's
trusted labs and let \(E_0\) purify the state supplied by Eve. Thus, without
loss of generality, the source can be written as a pure state
\[
    \lvert\Psi\rangle_{ABE_0},
    \qquad
    \rho_{AB}=\operatorname{Tr}_{E_0}\lvert\Psi\rangle\!\langle\Psi\rvert.
\]
For a signal label \(s\), Alice and Bob apply
\[
    U_s
    =
    \exp\!\left[
      i\phi_A^s \hat n_A+i\phi_B^s\hat n_B
    \right],
\]
and the global encoded state is
\[
    \lvert\psi_s\rangle
    =
    (U_s\otimes I_{E_0})\lvert\Psi\rangle.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Exact overlap formula]
For any two signal labels \(s,t\),
\begin{equation}
    \Lambda_{st}
    :=
    \langle\psi_s\vert\psi_t\rangle
    =
    \sum_{n_A,n_B\ge0}
      p_{n_A,n_B}
      e^{i\Delta\phi_A n_A+i\Delta\phi_B n_B},
    \label{eq:stage1-exact-overlap}
\end{equation}
where
\[
    p_{n_A,n_B}
    :=
    \langle n_A,n_B\vert\rho_{AB}\vert n_A,n_B\rangle,
\]
\(\Delta\phi_X=\phi_X^t-\phi_X^s\), and
\(\sum_{n_A,n_B}p_{n_A,n_B}=1\).
Equation~\eqref{eq:stage1-exact-overlap} is exact even if
\(\rho_{AB}\) has arbitrary Fock-basis coherences.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since \(U_s^\dagger U_t\) is diagonal in the joint Fock basis,
\begin{align}
    \Lambda_{st}
    &=
    \operatorname{Tr}
    \left[
      \rho_{AB}U_s^\dagger U_t
    \right] \\
    &=
    \sum_{n_A,n_B}
    \langle n_A,n_B\vert\rho_{AB}\vert n_A,n_B\rangle
    e^{i\Delta\phi_A n_A+i\Delta\phi_B n_B}.
\end{align}
All off-diagonal matrix elements of \(\rho_{AB}\) vanish from the trace.
\end{proof}

Thus the unknown source enters the Gram constraints only through a classical
probability distribution \(p_{n_A,n_B}\), even though the physical source
itself need not be phase randomized.

\subsection{Exact Gram constraints}

Represent Eve's channel and announcement by an isometry
\[
    V:\lvert\psi_s\rangle
    \longmapsto
    \sum_z \lvert z\rangle_Z\lvert e_s^z\rangle_E,
\]
where \(z\) is the public announcement and the
\(\lvert e_s^z\rangle\) are sub-normalized residual states. Isometry
preserves inner products, so
\begin{equation}
    \Lambda_{st}
    =
    \sum_z
      \langle e_s^z\vert e_t^z\rangle.
    \label{eq:stage1-gram-conservation}
\end{equation}
If \(G\) is the Gram matrix of all vectors
\(\{\lvert e_s^z\rangle\}_{s,z}\), then \(G\succeq0\),
Eq.~\eqref{eq:stage1-gram-conservation} is linear in \(G\), and the observed
probabilities are diagonal entries,
\[
    P(z\mid s)
    =
    \lVert e_s^z\rVert^2
    =
    G[(z,s),(z,s)].
\]
Consequently the pass probabilities, QBER constraints, and the phase-error
objective inherited from Eq.~(A7) of arXiv:1901.01942 are affine functions of
\(G\).

The exact optimization would therefore maximize the phase-error functional
over \(G\) and the infinite distribution \(p\), subject to
\[
    G\succeq0,
    \qquad
    p_{n_A,n_B}\ge0,
    \qquad
    \sum p_{n_A,n_B}=1,
\]
the overlap equalities
\eqref{eq:stage1-exact-overlap}--\eqref{eq:stage1-gram-conservation}, the
observed statistics, and the monitored energy constraints
\begin{equation}
    \mathbb E[n_A]\le\frac{\bar n}{2},
    \qquad
    \mathbb E[n_B]\le\frac{\bar n}{2}.
    \label{eq:stage1-energy-exact}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Rigorous photon-number truncation}

For computation define
\[
    S_N
    :=
    \{(n_A,n_B):n_A+n_B\le N\}
\]
and
\[
    \varepsilon_N
    :=
    1-\sum_{(n_A,n_B)\in S_N}p_{n_A,n_B}.
\]
Write
\[
    \Lambda_{st}
    =
    \Lambda^{(N)}_{st}+r_{st},
\]
where
\[
    \Lambda^{(N)}_{st}
    =
    \sum_{(n_A,n_B)\in S_N}
    p_{n_A,n_B}
    e^{i\Delta\phi_A n_A+i\Delta\phi_B n_B}.
\]
Because every discarded phase factor has modulus one,
\begin{equation}
    \lvert r_{st}\rvert
    \le
    \varepsilon_N.
    \label{eq:stage1-tail-overlap}
\end{equation}
Hence every physical attack satisfies
\begin{equation}
    \left|
      \sum_zG[(z,s),(z,t)]
      -
      \Lambda^{(N)}_{st}
    \right|
    \le
    \varepsilon_N.
    \label{eq:stage1-soc}
\end{equation}

The tail must also be charged to the energy budget. Since
\(n_A+n_B\ge N+1\) on \(S_N^c\),
\begin{equation}
    \sum_{S_N}p_{n_A,n_B}(n_A+n_B)
    +(N+1)\varepsilon_N
    \le
    \bar n.
    \label{eq:stage1-combined-energy}
\end{equation}
Dropping the nonnegative tail also gives the individually valid constraints
\begin{equation}
    \sum_{S_N}p_{n_A,n_B}n_A
    \le\frac{\bar n}{2},
    \qquad
    \sum_{S_N}p_{n_A,n_B}n_B
    \le\frac{\bar n}{2}.
    \label{eq:stage1-perarm-energy}
\end{equation}

Equations~\eqref{eq:stage1-tail-overlap}--\eqref{eq:stage1-perarm-energy}
define an \emph{outer relaxation}: every physical source obeying
\eqref{eq:stage1-energy-exact} maps to a feasible truncated point. Therefore
maximizing \(e_{\rm ph}\) over this relaxation gives a valid upper bound.  The
phase-error functional itself is justified for the arbitrary source model in
Section~\ref{sec:a7-general}.

\begin{remark}[Why the tail cannot be charged separately to both arms]
It would be unsound to add \((N+1)\varepsilon_N\) to each of the two
per-arm constraints. A discarded point such as \((0,N+1)\) has
\(n_A=0\), so there is no lower bound \(n_A\ge N+1\) on the discarded set.
Only the combined photon number has the required lower bound.
\end{remark}

\subsection{A first moment cannot control bunching}

For a one-mode distribution with fixed mean \(\mu>0\), consider
\begin{equation}
    P(N=0)=1-\frac{\mu}{K},
    \qquad
    P(N=K)=\frac{\mu}{K},
    \qquad K\ge\mu.
    \label{eq:stage1-two-point}
\end{equation}
Then \(\mathbb E[N]=\mu\), but
\begin{equation}
    g^{(2)}
    =
    \frac{\mathbb E[N(N-1)]}{\mathbb E[N]^2}
    =
    \frac{K-1}{\mu},
    \label{eq:stage1-unbounded-g2}
\end{equation}
which diverges as \(K\to\infty\). Thus no bound on \(g^{(2)}\), or on any
higher moment, follows from a mean-energy constraint alone.

This does not by itself prove the optimal QKD attack, but it proves the
structural point used later: the monitored first moment leaves arbitrarily
strong pulse-to-pulse concentration available to Eve.

Numerically, at
\[
    \bar n=10^{-2},
    \qquad L=0,
    \qquad N_{\max}=7,
\]
the current phase-error values are
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ccc}
\toprule
\(M\) & number of test states & \(e_{\rm ph}\) \\
\midrule
2 & 2 & 0.5000 \\
3 & 4 & 0.2865 \\
4 & 6 & 0.2573 \\
5 & 8 & 0.2572 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
so the test bases constrain the phase structure, but the distance remains
limited by source statistics. A representative optimizer has
\(g^{(2)}\sim36\).

\section{Stage 2: physical attenuation}
\label{sec:stage2-rigorous}

Let \(\mathcal L_\tau\) denote the trusted pure-loss channel of transmissivity
\[
    \tau=t^2.
\]
For simplicity the same \(\tau\) is written for both arms; the formulas
generalize immediately to \(\tau_A\ne\tau_B\).

\subsection{Phase covariance and the common mixed-state orbit}

Pure loss is phase covariant:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal L_\tau
    \!\left(
      e^{i\theta\hat n}\rho e^{-i\theta\hat n}
    \right)
    =
    e^{i\theta\hat n}
    \mathcal L_\tau(\rho)
    e^{-i\theta\hat n}.
    \label{eq:stage2-covariance}
\end{equation}
This follows directly from the beamsplitter dilation of pure loss.

Let \(T_A,T_B\) be the transmitted modes and retain Eve's original purifying
system \(E_0\) as a spectator. By covariance, the family arriving back at
the untrusted node has the form
\begin{equation}
    \rho_s^{TE_0}
    =
    W_s\rho_0^{TE_0}W_s^\dagger,
    \qquad
    W_s
    =
    e^{i\phi_A^s\hat n_{T_A}+i\phi_B^s\hat n_{T_B}}
    \otimes I_{E_0},
    \label{eq:stage2-unitary-orbit}
\end{equation}
for one state \(\rho_0^{TE_0}\) independent of \(s\).

\begin{proposition}[Common purification]
Choose a purification \(\lvert\chi\rangle_{TE_0R}\) of
\(\rho_0^{TE_0}\) and define
\[
    \lvert\chi_s\rangle
    =
    (W_s\otimes I_R)\lvert\chi\rangle.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
    \langle\chi_s\vert\chi_t\rangle
    =
    \operatorname{Tr}
    \left[
      \rho_0^{TE_0}W_s^\dagger W_t
    \right]
    =
    \operatorname{Tr}_{T}
    \left[
      \rho_0^{T}
      e^{i\Delta\phi_A\hat n_{T_A}
        +i\Delta\phi_B\hat n_{T_B}}
    \right].
    \label{eq:stage2-purified-overlap}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Use
\(\langle\chi|(X\otimes I)|\chi\rangle=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_0X)\).
The second equality follows because \(W_s^\dagger W_t\) acts trivially on
\(E_0\).
\end{proof}

This is the precise reason the mixed-state obstruction discussed in
Section~V of arXiv:2012.11104 does not directly apply here. Here the entire
mixed family is a known unitary orbit of one common state.

The auxiliary system \(R\) is a mathematical purification, not a physical
system available to Eve. Any physical attack on \(TE_0\) extends to
\(V\otimes I_R\). If the SDP optimizes over an arbitrary PSD Gram matrix
without enforcing that tensor-product restriction, it only gives Eve more
power. The resulting optimum is therefore an upper bound on the physical
phase error.

\begin{remark}[Why keeping the physical reflected port is the wrong
purification for this purpose]
If the reflected port of the attenuator is retained explicitly, the total
beamsplitter evolution is unitary and independent of \(s\), so the global
overlaps are exactly the pre-loss overlaps. The useful construction is to
trace out the trusted loss port first and then purify the reduced transmitted
state.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Exact overlap after thinning}

For one incoming Fock component \(\lvert m\rangle\),
\[
    N\mid m\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(m,\tau).
\]
Therefore
\begin{align}
    \mathbb E[e^{i\Delta\phi N}\mid m]
    &=
    \sum_{n=0}^{m}
      \binom{m}{n}
      \tau^n(1-\tau)^{m-n}
      e^{i\Delta\phi n} \\
    &=
    \left(
      1-\tau+\tau e^{i\Delta\phi}
    \right)^m.
    \label{eq:stage2-binomial-kernel}
\end{align}
For a two-arm input distribution \(p_{m_A,m_B}\),
\begin{equation}
    \Lambda^{(\tau)}_{st}
    =
    \sum_{m_A,m_B}
    p_{m_A,m_B}
    z_A^{m_A}z_B^{m_B},
    \qquad
    z_X
    :=
    1-\tau+\tau e^{i\Delta\phi_X}.
    \label{eq:stage2-exact-kernel}
\end{equation}

If the input is truncated to
\(S_N=\{m_A+m_B\le N\}\), define
\[
    \rho_{st}:=\max\{|z_A|,|z_B|\}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
    \left|
      \sum_{S_N^c}
      p_{m_A,m_B}z_A^{m_A}z_B^{m_B}
    \right|
    \le
    \varepsilon_N\,\rho_{st}^{N+1}.
    \label{eq:stage2-decaying-tail}
\end{equation}
When both relevant phase differences are nonzero,
\(|z_X|<1\) and this can be exponentially smaller than the bare tail
\(\varepsilon_N\). If one arm has \(\Delta\phi_X=0\), then
\(|z_X|=1\), so this uniform bound may revert to \(\varepsilon_N\).

\subsection{Loss does not control \(g^{(2)}\)}

For pure loss,
\[
    \mathbb E[N_{\rm out}]
    =
    \tau\mathbb E[N_{\rm in}],
\]
and
\[
    \mathbb E[N_{\rm out}(N_{\rm out}-1)]
    =
    \tau^2
    \mathbb E[N_{\rm in}(N_{\rm in}-1)].
\]
Therefore
\begin{equation}
    g_{\rm out}^{(2)}
    =
    g_{\rm in}^{(2)}.
    \label{eq:stage2-g2-invariance}
\end{equation}
Attenuation reduces the scale of the pulse but not its normalized bunching.

If the desired delivered mean is \(\mu\), then the incoming mean before
attenuation is \(\mu/\tau\), so a direct Fock representation necessarily
moves to photon numbers of order \(1/\tau\). This is why realistic
\(\tau\sim10^{-6}\) is computationally inaccessible on the original grid.

The current numerical Fock-grid results at
\[
    \bar n=10^{-2},\quad L=0,\quad M=4,\quad N_{\rm in}=60
\]
are
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{cc}
\toprule
\(\tau=t^2\) & \(e_{\rm ph}\) \\
\midrule
1.0 & 0.2567 \\
0.5 & 0.2293 \\
0.1 & 0.2094 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\section{Stage 3: strong-attenuation / continuum reformulation}
\label{sec:stage3-rigorous}

\subsection{Exact lattice measure before taking the limit}

Introduce the delivered energies
\[
    u_A=\tau m_A,
    \qquad
    u_B=\tau m_B.
\]
For a discrete input distribution \(p_{m_A,m_B}\), define its push-forward
measure
\begin{equation}
    \nu_\tau
    :=
    \sum_{m_A,m_B}
      p_{m_A,m_B}
      \delta_{(\tau m_A,\tau m_B)}.
    \label{eq:stage3-lattice-measure}
\end{equation}
It is supported on \((\tau\mathbb Z_{\ge0})^2\). The exact overlap is
\begin{equation}
    \Lambda^{(\tau)}_{st}
    =
    \int
      K_\tau(u_A,u_B)
      \,d\nu_\tau,
    \label{eq:stage3-exact-integral}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
    K_\tau
    =
    \prod_{X=A,B}
    \left[
      1-\tau(1-e^{i\Delta\phi_X})
    \right]^{u_X/\tau}.
    \label{eq:stage3-finite-kernel}
\end{equation}
The energy constraint becomes
\begin{equation}
    \int (u_A+u_B)\,d\nu_\tau
    \le
    \bar n.
    \label{eq:stage3-energy}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Controlled strong-attenuation limit}

Set
\[
    a_X:=1-e^{i\Delta\phi_X}.
\]
For \(\tau\le1/4\), \(|\tau a_X|\le1/2\). The estimate
\begin{equation}
    |\log(1-w)+w|
    \le
    |w|^2,
    \qquad |w|\le\frac12,
    \label{eq:stage3-log-bound}
\end{equation}
follows from the logarithm power series.

Define
\begin{equation}
    K_0(u_A,u_B)
    :=
    \exp[-u_Aa_A-u_Ba_B].
    \label{eq:stage3-limit-kernel}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Finite-\(\tau\) kernel error]
For \(u_A+u_B\le U\),
\begin{equation}
    |K_\tau-K_0|
    \le
    4\tau(u_A+u_B)e^{4\tau(u_A+u_B)}
    \le
    4\tau Ue^{4\tau U}.
    \label{eq:stage3-pointwise-error}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write \(K_\tau=K_0e^\delta\), where
\[
    \delta
    =
    \sum_X
    \frac{u_X}{\tau}
    \left[
      \log(1-\tau a_X)+\tau a_X
    \right].
\]
By Eq.~\eqref{eq:stage3-log-bound} and \(|a_X|\le2\),
\[
    |\delta|
    \le
    4\tau(u_A+u_B).
\]
Moreover \(\operatorname{Re}(a_X)=1-\cos\Delta\phi_X\ge0\), so
\(|K_0|\le1\). Using
\(|e^\delta-1|\le|\delta|e^{|\delta|}\) gives the result.
\end{proof}

If the measure is supported in \(u_A+u_B\le U\), integration and
Eq.~\eqref{eq:stage3-energy} give
\begin{equation}
    \left|
      \Lambda^{(\tau)}_{st}
      -
      \Lambda^{(0)}_{st}
    \right|
    \le
    4\tau\bar n\,e^{4\tau U}.
    \label{eq:stage3-integrated-error}
\end{equation}
Thus the finite-\(\tau\) error can be inserted explicitly as additional
overlap slack.

Dropping the lattice restriction and allowing any probability measure
\(\nu\) on \(\mathbb R_{\ge0}^2\) with the same moment constraints enlarges
the feasible set. Hence the exact continuum problem, supplemented by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:stage3-integrated-error}, is an outer relaxation of the
physical finite-\(\tau\) problem.

\subsection{Mixed-Poisson interpretation}

For one mode,
\[
    N\mid U=u\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(u)
    \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
    \mathbb E[e^{i\theta N}\mid U=u]
    =
    e^{-u(1-e^{i\theta})}.
\]
Thus the continuum model is a mixed-Poisson source:
\[
    U\sim\nu,
    \qquad
    N\mid U=u\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(u).
\]
A fixed coherent intensity is the special case
\(\nu=\delta_{u_0}\).

The factorial moments satisfy
\[
    \mathbb E[N]=\mathbb E[U],
    \qquad
    \mathbb E[N(N-1)]=\mathbb E[U^2],
\]
and therefore
\begin{equation}
    g^{(2)}
    =
    1+
    \frac{\operatorname{Var}(U)}{\mathbb E[U]^2}.
    \label{eq:stage3-mixed-poisson-g2}
\end{equation}
A first-moment monitor fixes \(\mathbb E[U]\) but leaves
\(\operatorname{Var}(U)\) unbounded.

\subsection{Continuum truncation and finite-grid certification}

For
\[
    D_U:=\{(u_A,u_B):u_A+u_B\le U\},
    \qquad
    \varepsilon_U:=1-\nu(D_U),
\]
the tail must satisfy
\begin{equation}
    \int_{D_U}(u_A+u_B)\,d\nu
    +
    U\varepsilon_U
    \le
    \bar n.
    \label{eq:stage3-tail-energy}
\end{equation}
The omitted overlap contribution has modulus at most \(\varepsilon_U\).

A second distinction is important. Replacing the continuous domain \(D_U\)
by a finite set of support points is an inner approximation and is not
automatically a rigorous security bound. A rigorous finite-grid
implementation may instead partition \(D_U\) into cells \(C_j\), retain the
cell masses \(q_j=\nu(C_j)\), and add a kernel-enclosure error. Since
\[
    \left|
      \frac{\partial K_0}{\partial u_X}
    \right|
    =
    |a_X|\,|K_0|
    \le
    |a_X|
    \le2,
\]
for a representative point \(u^{(j)}\in C_j\),
\begin{equation}
    |K_0(u)-K_0(u^{(j)})|
    \le
    |a_A|\,|u_A-u_A^{(j)}|
    +
    |a_B|\,|u_B-u_B^{(j)}|.
    \label{eq:stage3-cell-bound}
\end{equation}
Adding the weighted cell error to the overlap slack makes the grid an outer
approximation. Until that enclosure is implemented, the present continuum
grid should be treated as a numerical approximation rather than a fully
certified continuum bound.

At \(\tau=10^{-6}\), \(M=4\), and \(L=0\), the current scan is largest near
\[
    \bar n\simeq0.1,
    \qquad
    e_{\rm ph}=0.2348,
    \qquad
    R=6.89\times10^{-3},
\]
while the range cutoff remains around \(2\) km.

\section{Concentration attack: rigorous inequality and heuristic saturation}
\label{sec:concentration-rigorous}

Consider the two-point family
\begin{equation}
    \nu
    =
    (1-p)\delta_0+p\delta_{u_0}.
    \label{eq:stage3-concentration-family}
\end{equation}
Let \(q(u)\in[0,1]\) be the conditional probability of a pass announcement.

If vacuum gives no pass, \(q(0)=0\), then
\[
    P_{\rm pass}
    =
    p\,q(u_0)
    \le
    p.
\]
Thus \(p\ge P_{\rm pass}\). With the per-arm mean budget
\(\mu=\bar n/2\),
\[
    pu_0\le\mu,
\]
so
\begin{equation}
    u_0
    \le
    \frac{\bar n/2}{P_{\rm pass}}.
    \label{eq:stage3-concentration-exact}
\end{equation}
With vacuum pass probability \(q_0>0\),
\[
    P_{\rm pass}
    \le
    q_0+p(1-q_0),
\]
which gives
\begin{equation}
    p
    \ge
    \frac{P_{\rm pass}-q_0}{1-q_0},
    \qquad
    u_0
    \le
    \frac{\bar n}{2}
    \frac{1-q_0}{P_{\rm pass}-q_0},
    \label{eq:stage3-concentration-dark}
\end{equation}
provided \(P_{\rm pass}>q_0\).

These are rigorous upper bounds for the two-point family. The claim that Eve
can nearly attain them requires \(q(u_0)=O(1)\) for bright pulses. In that
regime \(p\approx P_{\rm pass}\).

For the honest weak-signal model,
\[
    P_{\rm pass}
    \simeq
    c\,\eta\,\bar n
\]
with a convention-dependent \(c=O(1)\). Then
\begin{equation}
    u_{0,\max}
    =
    O(\eta^{-1}).
    \label{eq:stage3-one-over-eta}
\end{equation}
The cancellation of \(\bar n\) is therefore a scaling statement derived from
the honest pass model.

For distinct phases,
\begin{equation}
    |K_0(u_0;\Delta\phi)|
    =
    e^{-u_0(1-\cos\Delta\phi)},
    \label{eq:stage3-bright-overlap}
\end{equation}
so bright components become exponentially distinguishable. This explains why
the optimized phase-error SDP moves toward \(1/2\) with distance. The logical
step from vanishing pairwise overlap to \(e_{\rm ph}\to1/2\), however, is not
a standalone theorem; it is an interpretation of the SDP optimum.

\section{Stage 4: per-pulse energy monitoring}
\label{sec:stage4-rigorous}

Stage~4 replaces a first-moment constraint by information about the support
of the delivered-energy distribution on accepted rounds.

\subsection{Ideal monitor as a quantum instrument}

Let the trusted energy monitor be a quantum instrument
\(\{\mathcal M_y\}_y\) acting before the secret basis and bit choices. For an
acceptance set \(W\), define
\[
    \mathcal M_{\rm acc}
    :=
    \sum_{y\in W}\mathcal M_y,
    \qquad
    P_{\rm acc}
    =
    \operatorname{Tr}\mathcal M_{\rm acc}(\rho).
\]
Conditioned on acceptance,
\begin{equation}
    \rho_{\rm acc}
    =
    \frac{\mathcal M_{\rm acc}(\rho)}
         {P_{\rm acc}}.
    \label{eq:stage4-conditioned-state}
\end{equation}
If acceptance is decided before, and independently of, the later basis and
bit choices, the security analysis may be conditioned on this event. The key
rate per accepted round is computed from \(\rho_{\rm acc}\); the rate per
original incoming pulse must be multiplied by \(P_{\rm acc}\).

For an ideal photon-number-diagonal monitor with
\[
    \Pi_W
    =
    \sum_{m:\,\tau m\in[u_-,u_+]}
      |m\rangle\!\langle m|,
\]
the accepted Fock distribution satisfies
\[
    p_m^{\rm acc}=0
    \quad\text{for}\quad
    \tau m\notin[u_-,u_+],
\]
so
\begin{equation}
    \operatorname{supp}\nu_{\rm acc}
    \subseteq
    [u_-,u_+].
    \label{eq:stage4-support}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Consequences of a support bound}

Take
\[
    u_\pm=u_\star(1\pm w),
    \qquad 0\le w<1.
\]
The concentration attack can no longer place accepted weight above
\begin{equation}
    U_{\rm accepted}
    \le
    u_\star(1+w),
    \label{eq:stage4-brightness-cap}
\end{equation}
so the \(1/\eta\) growth in Stage~3 is cut off by a distance-independent
bound.

Popoviciu's inequality gives
\begin{equation}
    \operatorname{Var}(U)
    \le
    \frac{(u_+-u_-)^2}{4}
    =
    w^2u_\star^2.
    \label{eq:stage4-popoviciu}
\end{equation}
Since \(\mathbb E[U]\ge u_\star(1-w)\),
\begin{equation}
    g^{(2)}
    \le
    1+\frac{w^2}{(1-w)^2}.
    \label{eq:stage4-g2-general}
\end{equation}
If feed-forward attenuation or calibration additionally fixes
\(\mathbb E[U]=u_\star\), then
\begin{equation}
    g^{(2)}
    \le
    1+w^2.
    \label{eq:stage4-g2-fixedmean}
\end{equation}

There is also a direct overlap-stability estimate. Define
\[
    f_\theta(u)
    =
    e^{-u(1-e^{i\theta})}.
\]
Since
\[
    |f_\theta'(u)|
    \le
    |1-e^{i\theta}|
    \le2,
\]
any accepted distribution supported in
\([u_\star(1-w),u_\star(1+w)]\) satisfies
\begin{equation}
    \left|
      \mathbb E[f_\theta(U)]
      -
      f_\theta(u_\star)
    \right|
    \le
    2wu_\star.
    \label{eq:stage4-overlap-stability}
\end{equation}
For two arms,
\begin{equation}
    |\Lambda_\nu-\Lambda_{\delta_{u_\star}}|
    \le
    |1-e^{i\Delta\phi_A}|\,w u_{\star,A}
    +
    |1-e^{i\Delta\phi_B}|\,w u_{\star,B}.
    \label{eq:stage4-twoarm-stability}
\end{equation}
Thus the energy window quantitatively confines the Gram overlaps to a
neighbourhood of the fixed-energy kernel.

\subsection{Finite-resolution monitor}

A real monitor can falsely accept an out-of-window pulse. Define
\begin{equation}
    \epsilon_{\rm mon}
    :=
    \Pr(U\notin[u_-,u_+]\mid\mathrm{acc}).
    \label{eq:stage4-monitor-leakage}
\end{equation}
Then
\[
    \nu_{\rm acc}
    =
    (1-\epsilon_{\rm mon})\nu_{\rm in}
    +
    \epsilon_{\rm mon}\nu_{\rm out}.
\]
Since \(|K_0|\le1\), the unknown out-of-window component contributes at most
\(\epsilon_{\rm mon}\) in modulus to every overlap. The SDP can therefore
retain an explicit monitor-tail variable and add overlap and energy slack,
in direct analogy with Stages~1 and~3.

This is the missing physical step in the present calculation: the numerical
window currently treats Eq.~\eqref{eq:stage4-support} as exact. A physical
security claim requires deriving \(\epsilon_{\rm mon}\) from a trusted
detector model and carrying it through the conditioning.

\begin{remark}[Rigour status of Stages 1--4]
The exact overlap identities, Stage~1 tail relaxation, attenuation kernel,
covariance/common-purification construction, finite-\(\tau\) continuum error,
mixed-Poisson moment identities, and ideal-monitor support implications above
have explicit derivations, including the general untrusted-source derivation
of the phase-error objective in Section~\ref{sec:a7-general}. Two ingredients
are not yet certified: (i) the replacement of the continuum by a finite
numerical grid unless the cell-enclosure slack in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:stage3-cell-bound} is implemented; and (ii) the conversion of a
real finite-resolution energy monitor into a quantitative
\(\epsilon_{\rm mon}\) bound. The current Stage~3--4 rate and range numbers
should be read with those qualifications.
\end{remark}


\section{Open points}

The main unresolved items are:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Rigorous post-selection argument.}
    The present SDP assumes that, after the local energy test, the conditional
    mixing measure for accepted rounds is supported inside the chosen energy
    window. This should be derived carefully as part of the security proof.

    \item \textbf{Certify the continuum discretisation.}
    The present logarithmic \(u\)-grid restricts the mixing measure to a
    finite support and is therefore an inner approximation. Implement the
    cell-wise enclosure of Eq.~\eqref{eq:stage3-cell-bound} so that every
    continuous mixing measure maps to a feasible finite-dimensional point.

    \item \textbf{Solver accuracy.}
    The strongest continuum/window calculations currently return
    \texttt{optimal\_inaccurate} with CLARABEL. Publication-quality scans
    should be rerun with MOSEK or another higher-accuracy solver.

    \item \textbf{Revisit the number of test bases.}
    The apparent saturation near \(M=4\) was found in a regime dominated by
    the concentration attack. Once that attack is removed by per-pulse
    monitoring, the optimal value of \(M\) should be re-optimized.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Working conclusion}

The main conceptual mechanism is now understood. Mean-energy monitoring does
not control rare bright pulses, and channel loss turns that freedom into a
post-selection advantage for Eve. Strong attenuation makes the mathematics
cleaner, but by itself it only rescales the attack. Per-pulse energy monitoring
directly removes the degree of freedom responsible for the range collapse and
is therefore the current leading route toward a useful plug-and-play
phase-matching QKD protocol.
